The problem we solve

Specialist knowledge your staff should not have to invent.

Most schools have capable Creative Arts teachers who are already carrying a full load. What they usually lack is not talent but current, competition-side knowledge: what this year's syllabus rewards, where entrants lose marks they did not know were available, and how a piece should be timed and presented.

That is exactly what an adjudicator has. I work as specialist support alongside your staff, not as a replacement for them.

Common questions

What governing bodies usually ask.

You adjudicate. Is that a problem for our entrants?

It is handled in writing and declared in advance. Every learner I coach goes on a register that reaches organisers before the event. At provincial and national level sections are judged by a panel of several adjudicators, so where a learner of mine is entered my mark is one of several rather than the deciding one; where I am sole adjudicator I do not judge my own clients. The full disclosure policy is published and forms part of the school contract.

Does online really work for a school squad?

For eisteddfod and speaking preparation, yes. The work is close-focus — interpretation, timing, voice, stillness — which is what a camera captures well. It also means a rural school can access the same coaching as a Pretoria one. Full-stage ensemble work is the exception, and I will say so.

Can you work alongside our existing drama teacher?

That is the intended arrangement. I work as specialist eisteddfod and speaking support alongside a school's own Creative Arts staff, rather than replacing them.

What about POPIA?

Learner information is processed under a written agreement with the school, held only as long as needed, and never shared. Session recordings go to the school and the learner's parents only. The privacy notice sets out the detail.
