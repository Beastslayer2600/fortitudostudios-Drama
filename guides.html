Guides

Preparation notes from the adjudicator's side of the table.

Guide 01

How to prepare a monologue

  1. Read the full piece twice before making any acting choices.
  2. Mark where the thought changes, not only where the sentence ends.
  3. Choose who you are speaking to and what you want from them.
  4. Rehearse standing up so your body learns the piece too.
  5. Polish the first ten seconds until the opening feels calm and controlled.
Guide 02

Eisteddfod performance checklist

  1. Know the title, author and time limit — and check them against this year's syllabus, not last year's.
  2. Practise your entrance, stillness, introduction and exit as carefully as the piece itself.
  3. Keep costume choices neat, readable and performance-friendly.
  4. Project to the back wall without shouting.
  5. Finish strongly, hold the final moment, then leave with confidence.
Guide 03

Audition confidence

  1. Arrive knowing your piece, your character and your first line.
  2. Use your breathing to slow down before you begin.
  3. Make bold choices, but keep them truthful.
  4. If you make a mistake, stay in character and continue.
  5. Thank the panel and leave the room professionally.
Guide 04

Writing a speech that scores

  1. Take the angle you can defend, not the one forty other entrants will also take.
  2. Argue, do not assert — every claim needs evidence and a stated link between them.
  3. Structure it as opening, escalation, turn and close.
  4. Write in your own voice. Language borrowed from an adult is audible from the back of the hall.
  5. Time it out loud at performance pace, not reading pace. They are not the same.
Guide 05

Managing nerves on the day

  1. Nerves are readiness, not a warning. The physical feeling is the same either way.
  2. Breathe out longer than you breathe in — four counts in, six out, four times.
  3. Do not rehearse the whole piece in the waiting room. Rehearse only the first line.
  4. Walk on slower than feels natural. Rushing on reads as fear before you have said a word.
  5. Find one point on the back wall and take a beat before you start.
Guide 06

For parents: what actually helps

  1. Ask them to perform it for you once a week, not once a night. Over-rehearsal flattens a piece.
  2. Praise a specific choice rather than the whole performance.
  3. Do not comment on the result of other entrants in front of your child.
  4. Read the adjudication sheet with them a day later, not in the car park.
  5. The comment that stings is usually the one worth working on.
