Why this is different

Anyone can write you a speech. Very few have marked one.

There is a real market in South Africa for written competition speeches, and most of it is written by capable writers who have never sat on an adjudication panel. They produce something well-argued and well-phrased that then loses marks for reasons nobody explains afterwards — a structure that peaks too early, an argument the learner cannot defend under questioning, language that is audibly not theirs.

I write from the other side of that table. I know how long a section runs, where attention drops, which rhetorical devices land with a panel and which read as showing off, and what happens in the questions afterwards.

The process

What happens after you order.

  1. A short briefFifteen minutes on Teams or a detailed WhatsApp. The theme, the section, the time limit, the learner's age and personality, and what they actually care about — that last one is what makes a speech sound like them.
  2. The angle, agreed firstBefore I write a word I send you the angle and the shape of the argument. If it is not right, we change it then, not after a full draft.
  3. The draftWithin 3–7 working days: the full speech, timed at performance pace, with delivery notes marked into the script — where to pause, where to lift, where to slow down.
  4. One revisionYou come back with anything that does not fit and I adjust. Most speeches need one small pass; some need none.
  5. Delivery coaching, if you took the packageTwo live sessions turning text into performance, recorded so the learner can see what a panel will see.

Questions

What parents ask.

Is it allowed to enter a speech somebody else wrote?

In most categories on the major South African circuits, yes — the published rules encourage learners to write their own material in the experimental and original-composition categories but do not require it generally, which is why a number of speech-writing services operate openly. The exceptions are those own-composition sections, and for those I coach rather than write. If you are ever asked who wrote the speech, answer honestly; there is nothing here that needs hiding.

You adjudicate. Isn't writing speeches a conflict?

It is an overlap, and I declare it. Every learner I coach and every speech I write goes on a register that reaches organisers before the event. At provincial and national level sections are judged by a panel, so my mark is one of several rather than the deciding one, and where I am sole adjudicator I do not judge my own clients. The full policy is published here.

Will it sound like my child wrote it?

That is most of the craft. I ask about their interests, how they actually speak, what makes them laugh, and what they have strong opinions about. A speech in vocabulary a twelve-year-old would not use is audible from the back of a hall and costs marks — so writing above the learner is a failure, not a flourish.

Could another entrant get the same speech?

No. Every speech is written once for one learner and never reused, including in another province or a later year.

What if we do not like the draft?

One round of revisions is included, and I agree the angle with you before writing so that a full rewrite should never be necessary. If the first draft genuinely misses, tell me and I will fix it.

How far in advance should we order?

As soon as the theme is announced. Three weeks before the competition is comfortable; the week before is possible but leaves no time to rehearse, which is where the marks actually are.
