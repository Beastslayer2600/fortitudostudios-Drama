The honest position

You can buy a speech online. You should not.

There is a whole industry selling ready-written speeches to South African learners. It is quick, it is cheap, and it is a poor deal. Most competition rules require the learner's own work, adjudicators can usually tell, and a student who did not write the words cannot defend them under questioning or recover when they lose their place.

I teach the learner to write it. They keep the skill, they own the argument, and they can stand behind every line. That is a harder product to sell and a far better one to buy.

The package

What R1,350 actually buys.

  1. Topic analysis and angleWe unpack the set theme together on Teams and find an angle the learner can genuinely defend — one that is theirs, not the obvious one forty other entrants will also use.
  2. Argument mappingClaim, evidence, and the link between them. This is the part most speeches get wrong: they assert rather than argue, and the marks reflect it.
  3. Structure and rhetoricOpening, escalation, turn and close. I teach the specific devices that work in competition — antithesis, tricolon, the deliberate pause — and when to leave them alone.
  4. The learner draftsThey write it. I give written feedback on each draft, marking where the argument thins, the rhythm stalls, or the language reaches beyond the learner's own voice.
  5. Two live coaching sessionsDelivery: pace, projection, eye contact, gesture, and what to do with your hands. Recorded so the learner can see what the audience sees.
  6. Competition-day preparationHandling nerves, the walk to the lectern, unprepared-speech technique, and answering the questions that follow.
